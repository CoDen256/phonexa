Feature model — how the 18 overlap
Passive place — where the constriction is (chart columns)
Exo-/Endo-labial, Dental, Alveolar, Post-alveolar, Alveolo-palatal (pre-palatal), Palatal, Velar, Uvular, Pharyngeal, Epiglottal, Glottal. One value, ever — it's a location.
Active articulator — what body part moves
Labial, Apical, Laminal, Sub-apical, Antero-dorsal, Postero-dorsal, Radical. Usually implied by manner+place; stored explicitly only when it disambiguates (ʂ apical vs ʃ laminal, both post-alveolar).
Secondary articulation — multi-select overlay
Labialized, Palatalized, Velarized, Pharyngealized, Glottalized, Aspirated. Zero, one, or several at once — Russian hard л carries both Velarized and Pharyngealized simultaneously.
Everything else
Voicing (binary, per symbol) · Syllabic (binary modifier) · Co-articulation (two simultaneous primary places, e.g. w = Labial+Velar) — orthogonal to all of the above.

Dental/Apical/Laminal from your list aren't a separate bucket — Dental is a passive place, Apical/Laminal are active articulators. Keeping those two axes apart is what removes the overlap.

Why a grid first: it's the fastest way to scan "who has what" across languages — e.g. toggle Palatalized and the whole Russian consonant system visibly doubles in the Alveolar/Labial columns, which is the actual shape of Russian's palatalization contrast. Weak point: dense cells (Alveolar fricatives) get crowded once 4+ languages are loaded at once.
Why a strip too: this is the better view for "what does Russian's /l/ actually look like" — the two variants sit side by side with their full feature readout, instead of being two small pills sharing one grid cell. Weak point: no cross-language comparison in this view — you'd flip tabs to see if German has the same contrast (it doesn't).
